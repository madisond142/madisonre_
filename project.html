Analytics Case Study

SQL & Relational Database Analytics

Designing a normalized relational database schema and formulating structured SQL queries to extract actionable business performance metrics and operational insights.

Role

Database & Analytics Designer

Focus Area

Business Analytics & ISDS

Core Tools

SQL & Relational Schema Design

Institution

LSU E. J. Ourso College

Objective

The Goal & Business Context

In enterprise operations, data is often scattered across disparate flat files or unorganized records, resulting in data redundancy, inconsistent reporting, and operational blind spots. The objective of this project was to architect a robust relational database from scratch and build structured SQL analytical queries that empower decision-makers with accurate, real-time insights.

Key analytical objectives:

  • Relational Architecture: Design an Entity-Relationship (ER) model representing core business entities (Customers, Orders, Inventory, Transactions) and enforce primary/foreign key integrity constraints.
  • Database Normalization: Structure relational tables to Third Normal Form (3NF) to eliminate update anomalies, remove redundant data, and ensure storage efficiency.
  • Complex SQL Querying: Formulate multi-table queries utilizing INNER/LEFT JOINs, GROUP BY aggregations, HAVING clauses, and subqueries to answer managerial business questions.
  • Decision Metrics: Extract critical performance indicators such as customer purchasing frequency, revenue breakdown by product line, and inventory turnaround rates.
Technical Stack

Tools & Concepts Applied

This project leveraged foundational database concepts and structured analytical query techniques.

SQL (Query Language)

Constructed DDL and DML statements, filtering, table joins, multi-level aggregations, and nested subqueries for reporting.

Entity-Relationship (ER) Modeling

Mapped real-world business entities, defined cardinalities (1:1, 1:N, M:N), and documented referential integrity rules.

3NF Normalization

Decomposed redundant unnormalized structures into Third Normal Form to prevent insert, update, and deletion anomalies.

Business Analytics & KPIs

Calculated revenue distributions, customer cohort behaviors, and sales volume trends to support operational strategy.

Analytical Methodology

The 5-Step Process

The project followed a structured systems analysis and database development lifecycle.

1

Business Requirements & Entity Identification

Analyzed business workflow requirements to identify essential data entities, business rules, and analytical reporting needs. Outlined entity attributes, identifying prospective primary keys and transaction timestamps.

2

Entity-Relationship Modeling (ER Diagrams)

Constructed conceptual and logical ER diagrams. Resolved many-to-many relationships using associative bridge tables and established clear foreign key references to protect referential integrity.

3

Normalizing to Third Normal Form (3NF)

Tested schemas for First Normal Form (atomic values), Second Normal Form (no partial dependencies on composite keys), and Third Normal Form (no transitive dependencies), ensuring clean, non-redundant database architecture.

4

Formulating Structured SQL Queries

Authored multi-table SQL queries joining customer, order, and product records. Utilized aggregate functions (SUM, AVG, COUNT), GROUP BY groupings, and HAVING filters to answer executive questions regarding revenue streams and transaction volumes.

5

Validation & Managerial Insights

Verified query accuracy against edge cases and null values. Translated structured query outputs into concise managerial takeaways and summary data points for business decision-makers.
